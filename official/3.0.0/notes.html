The units talk to each other over one UDP link instead of an MQTT broker. Nothing changes in how the clock is operated. Works only with units of MAJOR version 3: a unit of v2 refuses a Master of v3 and the other way round, so update every unit of a system, the Slave first.
Highlights4
One link between the unitsstate, report and configuration travel as UDP datagrams with a version, the system and the sender in one envelope. The Master sends every state three times (a broadcast, then unicast copies 25 and 75 ms later to the registered Slaves), so a lost datagram costs nothing. The embedded MQTT broker, PicoMQTT and PubSubClient are gone, and with them the TCP session states and reconnects. The _mqtt._tcp mDNS name stays so that units of v2 recognise the Master and refuse it by version.

Hardware verification4
On two units (red and blue, Matrix Portal S3 with a 64 × 64 panel), over WiFi, on the firmware of main at the time of the run (v2.3.0-28-g4f45382 and v2.3.0-31-g38305de), which is the firmware of this release up to documentation:
S4: hardware suite 17 passed (62 skipped); phone stress with 13 phones, no restart.
S5: real soak 2.5 hours with 3 phones and 7 power cuts: 2890 commands executed of 2892 sent, none twice, none lost (one overtaken by a later one), the game kept over 7 of 7 cuts, no unplanned restart, pair available 98.2 % including the cuts. One recovery after a cut took 127 s (the others 6 to 8 s). 2.9 % of the state datagrams the Mac heard by broadcast were missing; the Slave got every state by the unicast copies.
